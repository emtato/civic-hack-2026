CIVIC expands to City Innovation Via Internships and Co-op. The longer program places Waterloo students in Canadian municipalities for eight months. This event is a short version aimed at a practical municipal problem, with work shared with municipal audiences. A municipality should be able to see a plausible pilot path, and a person outside the team's discipline should be able to use and interpret the prototype.

## Eligibility and rules

The full Devpost rules, rather than generic platform badges, establish the following:

- Participants are students at or above the age of majority. Each team includes at least one currently enrolled University of Waterloo student; a solo participant therefore must be a UW student.
- A person may join one team; each team submits one project. FCI staff, judges and their immediate families cannot receive prizes. Student verification may be requested.
- Advance brainstorming and team formation are allowed. Bringing pre-built tools or project code written before launch is prohibited. Commits may be checked.
- Open-source code, libraries, datasets and AI tools are allowed with credit. Disclose AI assistance.
- Data must be public, open or organizer-provided. Personal or confidential data is excluded by the event rules.
- All building and edits stop at the deadline, including reports, videos and generation. Late entries are excluded.
- Teams retain ownership; entry licenses FCI and UW to present the hackathon version with credit. Rule or conduct violations can lead to disqualification.

Generic Devpost labels mention college students, professional organizations and geographic restrictions. The published full rules inspected here do not enumerate allowed countries. Do not invent a country list or resolve an exceptional eligibility situation without organizer clarification.

AI appears in the event tagline and tags. The inspected rules permit AI and require attribution; no particular model/provider or explicit mandatory AI component was identified. This observation is not an organizer ruling on an edge case.

## Submission and judging overview

Four required deliverables: repository link, 2–3 minute demo video, one-page Municipal Readiness Report, and short project description. A public hosted prototype link is welcome but optional. The challenge still requires a working proof of concept. Videos longer than three minutes are excluded.

Scoring totals 100: project 40, software 20, presentation 40. The report and video each receive 20. Round one examines videos and reports asynchronously, so the live finalist pitch cannot substitute for a complete initial submission. Full detail is in [judging and submission](judging-and-submission.md).

## Prizes and recognition

- Cash: first $750 CAD; second $500; third $250; total $1,500.
- Two non-cash recognitions: community-centered work reflecting residents/equity, and strong storytelling/visualization.
- Certificates/digital recognition and winner coverage through FCI channels are described.
- Winners may present to FCI staff, members and partners and receive an opportunity to interview with FCI. This is not a guaranteed internship or job offer.
