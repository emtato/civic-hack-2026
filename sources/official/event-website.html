CH-2Notice of vote
No. 2611 013

You'll be solving one of these challenges

Five municipal problems go to a vote at the 2026 State of Canada's Cities Summit by the Canadian Urban Institute, in Ottawa on Thursday 12 November. Voting closes at 3 pm. The winning problem is revealed at launch, so read all five now and come in with a user in mind.

  • 1Making municipal decisions easier to understandHow might we help residents understand a proposed municipal decision, how it could affect them, and when they can have a say?
    Full brief, PDF
  • 2Finding gaps in transit accessHow might we use public data to spot where transit schedules make it hard to reach essential destinations, and figure out which improvements matter most and what the trade-offs are, including first-mile/last-mile gaps?
    Full brief, PDF
  • 3Making long-term infrastructure costs visibleHow might we help residents weigh an infrastructure project's upfront price against its future maintenance, operating and environmental costs?
    Full brief, PDF
  • 4Tracking a city's commitmentsHow might we help residents see what progress a municipality has made on a public commitment, and what's still unclear?
    Full brief, PDF
  • 5From housing approval to constructionHow might we help residents and decision-makers understand what happens between a housing approval and construction, and where progress gets hard to track?
    Full brief, PDF

Municipalities across Canada face many of the same problems, so a problem solved well once can be shared, adapted and reused instead of rebuilt from scratch every time. The tool you build should be usable by someone outside your own discipline, and you'll be asked to explain it to them.

How it's judged

The judging panel scores every submission out of 100 points: 40 for the project, 20 for software design and code understanding and 40 for how it's presented. Open any criterion to see what a 1, a 3 and a 5 look like.

Part 1

Project

40 points
Problem understanding and stakeholders addressed10 points+
1 · Limited
  • The problem and affected stakeholders are unclear
  • Benefits or impacts are largely assumed.
  • The solution does not address the needs of the primary stakeholders.
3 · Meets expectations
  • Defines the problem and key stakeholders
  • Benefits and impacts are laid out and considers some differing needs or trade-offs.
  • The solution only addresses the needs of the primary stakeholders.
5 · Excellent
  • Clearly understands the problem and stakeholder needs beyond the surface level.
  • Clearly shows expected benefits and explains potential harms and trade-offs.
  • The solution addresses the needs of the primary stakeholders, plus additional stakeholders.
Project quality and functionality15 points+
1 · Limited
  • The core task cannot be completed, or outputs have major errors.
  • Key features are mainly simulated.
  • There are existing technical flaws that are not addressed.
3 · Meets expectations
  • The core task works on a relevant example
  • Main features are operational, some tertiary features are mocked up.
  • Some gaps, errors or workarounds remain.
5 · Excellent
  • The core workflow produces useful, checked outputs.
  • Main features are fully operational with very minimal placeholder aspects.
  • Results are reliable; data limits and incomplete features are clear. Responsible use of data and AI.
Design, clarity and feasibility10 points+
1 · Limited
  • Design could not be implemented in a real-world setting without substantial rework to address the municipal context.
  • A first-time user needs substantial help to navigate the tool or interpret its output.
3 · Meets expectations
  • Design could be implemented with some tweaks for the municipal context.
  • A first-time user can complete the main task with instructions, but encounters some confusing steps or terminology.
5 · Excellent
  • Design could be implemented right away in a municipal context.
  • A novice completes the main task independently, supported by clear navigation, plain language, accessible design and useful guidance.
Potential impact and practical value5 points+
1 · Limited
  • The connection to the challenge statement is unclear; a useful application is not established.
  • Design is not an improvement over what already exists or processes already in place.
  • Solution is specific to only one example municipal use case.
3 · Meets expectations
  • Clear connection to most aspects of the challenge statement. Connects the project to relevant priorities and identifies a plausible use and benefit for a host, sponsor or municipal partner.
  • Design is an incremental improvement over what already might exist.
  • Impact is strong but could be difficult to scale to use cases at other municipalities.
5 · Excellent
  • Demonstrates strong alignment with the problem identified in the challenge, a credible organizational use and clear practical value.
  • The potential impact is strong and would lead to substantial positive, real-world outcomes.
  • The presented solution/tool could be scaled to other municipalities.

5 · Excellent

CH-7Team rules

Team rules

  • SizeOne to four people.
  • WhoAt least one person on every team must be a University of Waterloo student. Any faculty or program.
  • MixMixed disciplines are strongly encouraged but not required. Planners and urban designers, engineers, developers and data scientists, designers, and policy, business and social science students all have a part. The best solutions come from teams that combine different perspectives.
  • FormingPre-formed teams are welcome. If you're on your own, use the team-finding channel on Discord once your invite arrives, and the team formation block at launch.
  • StartingNothing starts before launch. You can't bring pre-built tools or code written in advance, and commits will be checked. From 10 am Saturday, all building stops.
  • Your workYour team owns what it builds. By entering, you give FCI and the University of Waterloo permission to show, describe and present the hackathon version of your project, during the event and afterward, with credit to your team.
  • ConductCIVIC Hack is a welcoming, inclusive space. Everyone is expected to be respectful, collaborative and professional, and harassment or discrimination of any kind won't be tolerated. If something goes wrong, contact an organizer. The full code of conduct is below.

Questions

01Why join?+
Five reasons. You work on a real problem, chosen by municipal leaders rather than made up for the event. Your finished work is presented and shared with municipal leaders at the summit. There's $1,500 in cash prizes plus two special recognition awards. You get a look at a public-sector career, and a connection to FCI's CIVIC program. And you walk away with a project, a demo video and a story to tell.
02Who can take part?+
Teams of one to four, with at least one University of Waterloo student on each. You don't need to be a coder. We're looking for planners and urban designers, engineers, developers and data scientists, designers, and policy, business and social science students. The best solutions come from teams that combine different perspectives.
03Do I need a team before I register?+
No. Teams of one are allowed, and if you'd rather have company, register on your own, mark yourself as looking for a team, and use the team-finding channel on Discord once your invite arrives. There's also a team formation block at launch on Thursday.
04What do we actually build?+
A working proof of concept for the challenge that wins the summit vote: a tool a municipality could realistically pilot. It can be a web app, a data tool, a map, a dashboard or something else, as long as a municipal leader could use it without you in the room. This isn't only about writing code. It's about understanding a problem, designing for the people it affects and clearly explaining your thinking.
05Where does it happen?+
Entirely online. Registration, submissions and judging are on Devpost. Teams, help, mentors and announcements are on Discord, and you'll receive the invite by email after registration. Launch and the Saturday showcase are live sessions, and the links go out by email and on Discord.
06Do I have to go to Ottawa?+
No. The 2026 State of Canada's Cities Summit is where municipal leaders vote on the challenge, in front of a live audience. You take part in the hackathon online from wherever you are.
07When is the challenge revealed?+
At launch, Thursday 12 November at 6 pm EST. The summit vote closes at 3 pm the same day. The five candidates are listed above, so you can think through all of them beforehand.
08What support is there during the event?+
Mentors, technical and municipal experts available during office hours to help you get unstuck. A livestreamed panel on municipal AI from the summit on Friday morning, with the recording posted afterward. Video resources on design thinking, pitching and demo videos, recorded so you can watch any time. And a help desk: organizers on Discord throughout the event, including overnight.
09What does "hacking ends" mean?+
At 10 am on Saturday all building stops, whatever kind of building it is: writing code, generating code, editing the report, re-cutting the video. Your submission has to be in on Devpost by then. Late submissions are not considered.
10Does it cost anything?+
No. Registration is free and open until launch.
11What happens after the event?+
Winners are announced on FCI's website, newsletter and LinkedIn, and invited to present to FCI staff, members and partners. The project gallery and recordings are published afterward.

Code of conduct

CIVIC Hack is about building better cities together. We want everyone, whatever their school, program, background or experience level, to feel welcome and safe taking part. This code applies to all participants, mentors, judges, organizers and partners in every event space: Discord, Devpost, video calls, livestreams and any direct messages between participants.

  • Be welcoming and respectful

    • Treat everyone with respect, especially people new to hackathons or from different backgrounds and disciplines.
    • Give constructive feedback, and take feedback in good faith.
    • Respect people's time and boundaries, including when they say no to joining a team or chatting.

  • Unacceptable behaviour

    • Harassment, intimidation or bullying of any kind.
    • Discriminatory comments or jokes about race, ethnicity, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, religion, age, appearance or any other personal characteristic.
    • Sexual language, imagery or advances, in public channels or private messages.
    • Unwanted private messages after someone has asked you to stop.
    • Sharing someone's personal information without their consent.
    • Recording, screenshotting or sharing other people's video, audio or private messages without consent.
    • Disrupting talks, livestreams, calls or channels, including spam and trolling.
    • Hateful, violent or explicit content in usernames, profile pictures or project submissions.

  • Honest work

    • Submit work your team built during the hackathon. Credit any open-source code, datasets and AI tools you used.
    • Use only public, open or organizer-provided data. Don't use personal or confidential information.
    • Don't plagiarize other teams' work.

  • Reporting

    If you experience or see behaviour that breaks this code, or if anything makes you feel unsafe, please tell us:

    • Direct message any member of the organizer team on Discord.
    • Email mhains@uwaterloo.ca. Preethi Raghuraman and Krishnam Jhalani are copied.

    Reports are handled as confidentially as possible. You won't face retaliation for reporting in good faith.

  • Consequences

    Organizers may take any action they consider appropriate, including a warning, removal from Discord or event sessions, disqualification from prizes, or removal from the hackathon. Serious incidents may be referred to the relevant university under its policies, or to the appropriate authorities.

By participating in CIVIC Hack, you agree to follow this code of conduct.

Tips for your demo video

Your video doesn't need to be highly produced. We need to understand the problem, see your solution working and know how a municipality could use it.

What to show

  • Start with the problem.In the first 20 seconds, say which challenge you're tackling, who it affects and what goes wrong for them today.
  • Show your solution working within 90 seconds.Don't spend the first half of your video on background.
  • Walk through one task from start to finish.Show it from the point of view of a resident or municipal staff member instead of touring every feature.
  • Show what changes.Make the difference between how things work today and how they'd work with your solution obvious.
  • Name your data.Tell us which datasets you used, where they came from and what assumptions you made.
  • Be upfront about what's real.Tell us what works, what doesn't and what's mocked up. Sample data is fine if you say you're using it.
  • End with the path forward.Explain the impact your solution could have and what a municipality would need to put it to use.

How to make it

  • Keep it to three minutes.If it runs long, cut features. Don't rush your narration or speed up the audio.
  • Record your screen and narrate over it.Show the solution itself, using slides only for brief context if you need them.
  • Write a short script in your own words.Read it out loud and time it before you record.
  • Use tools you already know.Zoom, Microsoft Teams or PowerPoint will all do the job, so this isn't the time to learn new editing software.
  • Make it easy to hear.Record somewhere quiet, use your team's best microphone and listen to a short test clip first.
  • Add captions.Auto-captions are a good start, but correct any errors, especially names and technical terms.
  • Protect privacy and respect copyright.Don't show anyone's personal information, and don't use music or images you don't have permission to use.

Before you submit

  • Leave at least three hours before the 10 am Saturday deadline.That gives you time to record, polish and upload, and uploads alone can take several hours.
  • Test your link.Open it in a private browser window while signed out to make sure judges can watch it.
